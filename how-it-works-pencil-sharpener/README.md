# How it works: why your pencil tip snaps in the sharpener

Episode 5 of "How ___ actually works". Sources at the bottom.

## What the blade does

- In a handheld sharpener the blade sits in the cone at an angle. Turning the pencil shaves off one thin spiral strip; the cone sets the shape of the point ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pencil_sharpener)).

## Why the tip snaps

- A sharp blade slices the graphite core. A **dull blade catches it**, and the tip breaks off ([Blackwing sharpener FAQ](https://blackwing602.com/pages/blackwing-sharpener-faq)).
- An **off-centre core** snaps too: one side of the point ends up with almost no wood around the lead.

## The crank sharpener is a planetary gearbox

- A crank sharpener uses helical cutters. Each cutter has a small gear that runs inside a fixed ring gear, so the cutters **orbit the pencil and spin at the same time** — a planetary gear set ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pencil_sharpener), [US 839,806](https://patents.google.com/patent/US839806), [US 2,515,174](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2515174A/en)).
- An early design with an orbiting cutter and a shavings chamber: [J. L. Love, US 594,114](https://patents.google.com/patent/US594114).
