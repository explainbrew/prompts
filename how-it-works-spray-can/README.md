# How it works: there's no pump in a spray can

Episode 4 of "How ___ actually works". Sources at the bottom.

## What pushes the paint

- The propellant is a **liquefied gas**. Its vapour, above the liquid, presses down and pushes the paint up the dip tube ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerosol_spray), [Explain that Stuff](https://www.explainthatstuff.com/aerosolcans.html)).
- Typical can pressure is about **3–5 bar** (45–75 psi) ([edding FAQ](https://faq.edding.com), [WEICON](https://blog.weicon.de)).

## The button

- Pressing the actuator opens a **spring valve**. The mix leaves the nozzle, the propellant flashes back to gas and tears the paint into a fine mist ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerosol_spray)). A classic valve design: [US 2,631,814](https://patents.google.com/patent/US2631814A/en).

## The rattle

- The rattle is a **mixing ball** that breaks up pigment settled at the bottom ([US 3,087,707](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3087707A/en)). Labels tell you to keep shaking for about a minute after the rattle starts.
